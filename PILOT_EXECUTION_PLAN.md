# C1 Deutsch — Four-Week Pilot Execution Plan

## Purpose

This plan is ready for a real learner pilot. It does not claim that a pilot has already happened and does not convert app scores into CEFR or examination predictions.

## Evidence rules

The pilot records practice evidence, not a formal language certificate. Self-reflection, automatic quiz results, synthetic-TTS listening and human review must remain separate evidence sources. A missing score is recorded as **not measured**, never as zero. Speaking, pronunciation and interaction claims require an actual human or appropriately validated acoustic/interaction procedure.

## Weekly protocol

| Week | Focus | Required evidence | Delayed follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 / baseline | Baseline profile, consent, device/audio check, one general and one chosen-context task | Baseline attempt IDs, context, task version, learner confidence, reviewer availability | Repeat the same dimensions only after the planned delay |
| 1 | Retrieval, explanation, listening transcript/TTS practice, one controlled production | At least three independent retrieval attempts, one unfamiliar transfer attempt, error notes | Review selected items after 1 day and 3 days |
| 2 | Interaction, repair, register and feedback challenge | One role-switch dialogue, one real-life entry, one challenged model feedback item | Revisit the same weakness in a new context after 7 days |
| 3 | Mediation, argumentation and spontaneous response | One mediation response, one written argument, one time-limited spontaneous response | Human review where available; otherwise mark dimensions unmeasured |
| 4 / checkpoint | Unfamiliar transfer and retention after pause | New general task plus new chosen-context task, human rubric if available | Compare with baseline descriptively; no CEFR conversion |

## Minimum weekly record

For each activity record:

- UTC timestamp and local date;
- stable unit ID and task ID;
- context track;
- task version and whether the task was unfamiliar;
- response type: text, audio response, role-play, quiz or reflection;
- assistance status, including model reveal or rereading;
- learner confidence before and after, on a clearly labelled 1–5 scale;
- observed error or repair note;
- evidence source: self-reflection, automatic quiz or human review;
- next planned review date;
- consent status for any stored recording or human review.

## Success indicators

These are pilot indicators, not validated outcomes:

1. At least 80% of scheduled sessions are completed or explicitly marked as missed.
2. At least four delayed retrieval events are completed after the initial exposure.
3. At least two unfamiliar-transfer tasks are attempted without the model shown first.
4. Every stored human review contains all ten rubric dimensions or explicitly records a dimension as not measured.
5. No reviewer, learner or product note reports synthetic TTS as native-speaker evidence.
6. Any safety-sensitive, medical, legal, employment or personal-data scenario is labelled as fictional practice or routed to appropriate professional advice.

## Analysis template

At the end of week 4, compare baseline and checkpoint by task family and evidence source:

- completion and retention rates;
- response latency where reliably measured;
- number and type of repaired errors;
- transfer-task independence;
- human-review dimension scores, only when a reviewer actually supplied them;
- learner-reported usefulness and burden;
- audio/device failures and privacy concerns.

Report absolute observations and uncertainty. Do not report “C1 achieved”, examination readiness, pronunciation improvement or causal learning effects from this pilot alone.

## Operational prerequisites

Before starting with a learner, verify: valid APK installation on a physical device, microphone permission flow, German TTS availability, audio focus, Room persistence after process death, export/delete behavior, consent wording, reviewer identity process and a secure location for any shared pilot records.
